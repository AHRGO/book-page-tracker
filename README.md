# book-page-tracker
A tool to track your progress, plus, where you stopped in one long activity.

# A bit of context.
I'm a reader. In fact, I'm more likely to be called a bookworm. And many times, I've started more than one book at a time.

Since my youth I've been doing this. I love the challenge of change contexts (books) and then recall everything I read of this one book, before switched to another one.

In general, I tried to read the maximum of two books at once, but in recent years I've found a pleasure in reading techinical books to learn things that I was curious about and/or I needed to understand in order to become a better developer.

# The problem
Here's the thing: sometimes, one book got more exciting than other (usually the story ones over the technical ones), and then I completely forgot about that other book I was reading.

To make things more interesting, I read a lot of PDF's. Sometimes entire books, sometimes papers, or whatever another piece of knowledge that my curiosity drives me through.

Also, as daily life goes and priorities change, I've needed to focus on some things and let others aside, mainly the things I was studying only by curiosity. When I did what I had to, I come back to my delightful readings and, time and time again, I've asked myself the same painful quesiton:

"Where did I stop in this one?".

# The solution
I've created a google sheets to track down what page I've stopped before switching contexts and it turns that it worked really good. And since I've tracking ***where I was in some reading***, I could tell my progress at it, and by this, inspire myself to keep going.

Fun fact: over time, I've started to track every other things that I could track where I've stopped: technical courses, series, youtube playlists and stuff like that.

# The results
By doing this, I've been able to finish more books and courses by not needing to re-read/re-watch everything from the beggining. 

I've also been able to, when finishing a book or course, pick the one with the least progress, lessering the time span between the switch of context, which help me to keep understand/learn better.

This spreadsheet also improved my motivation, helping me to continue reading as I see progress in things I'm doing and have a metric of how much I've accomplished.

# This project
This app intends to be the same as that spreadsheet. Simple as that. 

Maybe I add something cooler along the way that the spreadsheet doesn't allow me? Perhaps. 

For now, I'll keep it simple.
